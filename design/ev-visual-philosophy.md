# Signal Cabinet, Daylight

Daylight is the white counterpart to the Signal Cabinet that carries the profile. The discipline is unchanged — a curated instrument panel rather than decoration — but the field inverts. Where the cabinet holds instruments against smoked black, Daylight lays them on paper: surfaces are white, edges are thin, and every module earns its place by carrying information rather than filling space. Restraint is the point. A tool whose subject is discretion cannot advertise itself with noise.

Colour is reduced to two voices, and both are load-bearing. Red marks anything that must not escape: secrets found, sessions quarantined, the moment a rule fires. Blue marks the sanctioned path: data in motion, verified payloads, work that is safe to continue. Nothing is coloured for mood. If a reader learns only that red means danger and blue means flow, they can read every diagram in this repository without a legend, and the legend that exists is a courtesy rather than a crutch.

Motion is evidence, not ornament. Packets travel the rail because the pipeline is a sequence; red packets stop at the scrub stage because that is literally where they stop; the quarantine diversion fires on a long cycle because quarantine is rare, and animating it as often as the happy path would misrepresent the system. Status lights breathe at offset intervals so the panel feels alive under inspection without ever competing with the labels. Every loop must remain tolerable after the twentieth viewing, which rules out anything that flashes, bounces, or demands attention it has not earned.

Typography behaves as instrumentation. Monospace carries identifiers, counts, section markers and anything the reader might compare against a terminal; the humanist sans carries the short explanations underneath. Labels are set in coordinates rather than sentences, letter-spaced when small so they read as engraving on a panel. Numbers are given room and weight, because in this project the numbers are the argument — 381, 160, 4, 248 are the evidence that the tool ran against something real.

Diagrams are hand-written SVG with SMIL and no build step, matching a codebase that carries zero dependencies. Nothing is generated from a chart library, nothing pulls a font from a CDN, and every file can be opened, read, and corrected by a person. The work should feel like an engineer and an interface designer are the same careful person: dense enough to reward a second look, honest enough that the picture and the source agree, and quiet enough that the reader trusts it.
